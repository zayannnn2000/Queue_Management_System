**Scenario** 

A clinic or salon wants a simple token-based queue management platform to manage walk-in customers and scheduled appointments. Staff need a clear view of the queue, while customers should be able to understand their position and estimated waiting time. 

**Requirements** 

Build a working web application that allows staff to:   
● Add customers to the queue as walk-ins or scheduled appointments.   
● View the current queue, including customer names, token numbers, and booking types.   
● Identify and call the next customer.   
● View the estimated waiting time for customers in the queue. 

The application should also give customers a view of their own position and estimated waiting time. A public queue or status view is sufficient. No customer login is required. The platform must handle both walk-ins and appointments. The brief requires customers to be served fairly and in order, while also ensuring appointments are served according to their scheduled times. This tension is deliberate. No service duration data or detailed queue-prioritization rules are provided, and the two goals can conflict. Use your judgment, clearly document any assumptions you make, and explain the decisions and trade-offs behind your queue-handling logic and waiting-time calculation. 

**Development Guidelines**

Build a functional, interactive application rather than a static mockup.   
● Use realistic but fictional sample data. Never use real customer names or personal data.   
● Ensure the interface is intuitive and suitable for clinic or salon staff.   
● Deploy the application to Vercel so it can be accessed through a public URL. If Vercel is genuinely unavailable to you, any free public host is acceptable. Note which host you used and why.   
● A working, clearly reasoned simple application is better than a beautiful but unfinished one. 

**Deliverables** 

● Live application: The deployed public URL, recorded inside your PDF as described in the Submission Instructions.   
● Source code: A link to a public repository, such as GitHub or GitLab.   
● Brief documentation: One to two pages, included as a section of your PDF, covering your key design decisions, assumptions, queue-handling logic, and waiting-time calculations.   
● AI Usage Log: The prompts used to build and debug the application, what you accepted or changed, and how you tested the resulting behavior. Include this as a section of your main AI Usage Log.
